Everything Euphoria does.

From the first analysis to the last transition of the night. Pick an area of the app to see it, what it does and where to learn more.

Library and analysis

Add a folder and Euphoria reads every track once: its key, its tempo, its energy and its audio quality, cached so the next launch is instant.

Keys show in Camelot and in musical notation, tempos come from the beat grid, and energy uses Euphoria's own documented scale from 1 to 10.

Select a track and the inspector shows its place on the Camelot wheel, its waveform with cues, what else its key and tempo could be, and the tracks that mix well with it.

⌘OAdd a folder⌘RRescan

What you can do

  • Filter by tempo, energy, quality and stage
  • Sort and resize every column
  • See the tracks that mix well with the selected one
  • Look for new and removed files with one rescan

Needs review

A key or tempo rated low confidence, a key that disagrees with the file's tags, or a file that could not be analyzed lands in one list.

Each track says why it is there: Key unsure, Tempo unsure, or a key that differs from the one in its tags.

Play the notes of the key on the keyboard in the inspector and hear which fit, shift the key to hear what it mixes with there, then correct it in one click.

What you can do

  • Check a key by ear with the marked notes
  • See the runner-up key and tempo
  • Settle a track once it is right
  • Corrections are used by every export

Command palette

One box finds tracks and runs commands. It understands what a DJ types: 8A 124 deep finds tracks in 8A or a key that mixes with it, near 124 BPM, with deep in the name.

Type a key, a tempo, a tempo range or an energy, mixed with words in any order. The exact key comes first, then one step on the wheel and the relative key.

Under the tracks, every menu command is listed with its shortcut, so the palette teaches the keyboard as you go.

⌘KOpen the palette

Focus view

The focus view gives the selected track the whole window: its sections, a zoomed waveform with cues, its energy and where each stem sounds, on one time axis.

Drag cue flags onto beats, draw a loop with Command held, and change the playing tempo with key lock. Nothing is written to the file.

Pick a track in Mixes into and the Plan card writes the best transition in a sentence: which sections to mix, how many bars, where to swap the bass, and at what tempo.

Decks and mashups

Two decks under the table, with sync, key lock, loops and a crossfader, so you can hear a transition before you play it out.

Each deck shows its key, its tempo, its waveform with bars and cues, and its energy over time. Playback lines up the downbeats of both decks.

Next on deck B lists what mixes well after deck A, ranked by key move, tempo and the energy you want next. Mashup mode swaps it for tracks that layer over deck A, with a key shift and stems.

⌘⇧DShow or hide the decks

Set builder and playlists

Draw the energy you want across the night, choose a pool of tracks and a length, and Euphoria orders a set along the curve with every mix in a compatible key.

Each track becomes a bar on the curve, so you see at once where the set fits and where it does not. A chip row sums up how far it strays, how many rules it breaks and how long it runs.

Lock tracks, reorder them, play any transition, then render the whole set as one continuous mix or save it as a playlist with its key path, tempo range and energy flow.

Live

A small panel stays on top and shows the key and tempo of whatever the Mac is playing, with the tracks of your library that mix in.

Play a track in rekordbox, Spotify or a browser, or a turntable through an audio interface, and Live names its key, tempo and energy as it plays. Nothing is recorded.

It keeps running in the menu bar when you close the window.

Stems

Separate a track into its four stems with the Demucs model, then play the full track, the acapella or the instrumental, or any stem alone.

Stems feed the lanes of the focus view and the planner, which use them to find windows where two tracks never sing or play basslines at once.

Export stems, the acapella or the instrumental as WAV files, also in a shifted key.

Tags

Write key, tempo and energy into the tags your DJ software reads, or edit title, artist, genre and other fields across thousands of files at once.

Every file and every field is listed before anything is written. Keys and tempos already in your files are kept, and unsure values are skipped.

Undo puts the old tags back byte for byte, from the app or with euphoria tags undo.

Audio quality

Euphoria checks the codec, the real bit rate and where the content stops, and flags files likely re-encoded from a lower quality source.

euphoria quality lists the files to replace, worst first, with the reason: low bit rate, fake lossless or damaged. It writes the list as CSV or as an M3U8 playlist too.

Prepare files writes copies that play evenly on a club system: to a loudness target, under a true-peak ceiling, with tuning corrected if you want.

What you can do

  • Real bit rate and cutoff, per file
  • Fake lossless detection

Exports and command line

Analyse once, then send keys, tempos, beat grids, cues and playlists to the library you play from, from the app or the command line.

$ euphoria analyze ~/Music/"DJ tracks" --json
      $ euphoria quality ~/Music --m3u8 replace.m3u8
      $ euphoria export rekordbox ~/Music --beat-grid --cues
      $ euphoria export traktor ~/Music --beat-grid --cues
      $ euphoria tags write --serato-markers ~/Music
      $ euphoria tags undo

rekordbox gets an XML file with keys, tempos, energy, saved loops and a playlist per folder. Traktor gets an NML collection. Serato, Engine DJ and VirtualDJ get their cues and grids written in, after a copy is saved.

Every export and analysis is also a command, with table or JSON output you can script.
